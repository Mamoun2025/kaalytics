What is Predictive Maintenance?

Predictive maintenance is an approach that uses data and analytics to forecast when equipment is at risk of failure. Unlike preventive maintenance (based on fixed intervals) or reactive maintenance (after a breakdown), predictive maintenance intervenes exactly when needed.

The 3 Types of Maintenance:

  • Reactive: Repair after breakdown (costly, unpredictable)
  • Preventive: Intervention at fixed intervals (may be premature or delayed)
  • Predictive: Intervention at the right time (optimal)

How Does It Work?

Predictive maintenance operates on a 4-step cycle:

  1. Data Collection: Sensors installed on vehicles continuously measure key parameters (temperature, vibration, pressure, fuel consumption, etc.)
  2. Transmission: This data is sent to a central platform via cellular or satellite connections
  3. Analysis: Machine learning algorithms analyze the data and detect anomalies or concerning trends
  4. Action: The system generates alerts and intervention recommendations before failure occurs

Technologies Used

Predictive maintenance relies on several key technologies:

  • IoT Sensors: Accelerometers, thermocouples, pressure sensors, engine hour meters
  • Connectivity: 4G/5G, LoRa, satellite for remote areas
  • OBD-II: Standard interface for accessing engine data
  • Machine Learning: Learning algorithms for anomaly detection
  • Cloud Computing: Computing power to process large data volumes

Concrete Benefits

Reduced Downtime

Unplanned failures are significantly reduced

Lower Maintenance Costs

Significant savings on repairs and parts

Improved Availability

Higher vehicle fleet availability rates

Extended Lifespan

Equipment lasts longer with proper maintenance timing

Implementation: Where to Start

Implementing a predictive maintenance solution happens progressively:

  1. Phase 1 – Audit: Evaluate your current maintenance costs and identify critical equipment
  2. Phase 2 – Pilot: Deploy on a representative sample (10-20% of the fleet)
  3. Phase 3 – Calibration: Fine-tune alert thresholds with field feedback
  4. Phase 4 – Rollout: Gradually expand to the entire fleet
  5. Phase 5 – Optimization: Continuous improvement of predictive models
